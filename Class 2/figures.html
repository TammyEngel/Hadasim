People of the Titanic

Meet the people behind the historic voyage.

The Captain The Designer Passengers and Crew

Captain Edward Smith

Edward John Smith was the captain of Titanic. He had spent many years working at sea and had commanded other passenger ships for the White Star Line. His experience made him a familiar figure among travelers who regularly crossed the Atlantic. On Titanic's first voyage, he was responsible for the ship, its crew, and the people traveling aboard.

A captain depended on many other people to operate such a large vessel. Officers supervised navigation, lookouts watched the sea, and engineers maintained the machinery below deck. After the collision, Smith faced an emergency that required distress messages and the evacuation of passengers. He died during the disaster. His story reminds visitors of the enormous responsibility carried by those in command.

What was the captain responsible for?

The captain had overall responsibility for navigation, safety, and the management of the ship and its crew.

Thomas Andrews

Thomas Andrews was a shipbuilder who played an important role in Titanic's design. He worked for Harland and Wolff, the company that built the ship in Belfast. He traveled on the maiden voyage as part of a team that observed how the new liner performed and identified improvements that might be needed.

After Titanic struck the iceberg, Andrews helped assess the damage. His knowledge of the ship's structure allowed him to understand the seriousness of the flooding. He recognized that the ship would sink because too many compartments had been damaged. Accounts of the disaster describe him encouraging passengers to put on life jackets and move toward the lifeboats. Andrews did not survive.

Why was the designer aboard?

Andrews and his colleagues were checking the ship during its first voyage and looking for problems that needed attention.

Passengers and Crew

Different Lives, One Voyage

Titanic's passengers came from different countries and social backgrounds. Some wealthy travelers stayed in luxurious first-class cabins. Other passengers traveled in second or third class. Many third-class passengers were emigrants hoping to find work or join family members in North America. Their journeys were connected by a shared destination, although their experiences aboard were very different.

The People Who Kept the Ship Running

The crew included stewards, cooks, wireless operators, engineers, and many other workers. Their daily tasks made the voyage possible. During the emergency, wireless operators transmitted calls for assistance, while other crew members helped prepare and lower lifeboats. Remembering Titanic means remembering these workers and ordinary passengers as well as its famous figures. Each person had a life beyond the ship, with relationships, responsibilities, and plans interrupted by the disaster.

Were all passengers traveling for the same reason?

No. Some traveled for pleasure or business, while others were moving abroad to begin a new chapter in their lives.
