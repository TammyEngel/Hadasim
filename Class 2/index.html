Building the Titanic

The story of the Titanic began with the growing demand for travel across the Atlantic Ocean. In the early twentieth century, ocean liners carried travelers, workers, and families between Europe and North America. The White Star Line wanted to attract passengers with large ships that offered comfort and luxury. Titanic was designed as part of this plan, alongside its sister ships, Olympic and Britannic.

Construction began in Belfast, Ireland, in 1909 at the Harland and Wolff shipyard. Thousands of workers helped build the enormous vessel. Its steel hull contained watertight compartments intended to limit flooding if damage occurred. Inside, the ship offered dining rooms, lounges, and cabins for different passenger classes. First-class passengers enjoyed especially elegant surroundings, while third-class accommodation served many people seeking a new life abroad.

Why was the Titanic built?

The White Star Line wanted a large, comfortable liner that could compete for passengers on the busy route between Europe and North America.

The First Voyage

On April 10, 1912, Titanic departed from Southampton, England, on its maiden voyage. Its destination was New York City. Before crossing the Atlantic, the ship stopped at Cherbourg in France and Queenstown, now called Cobh, in Ireland. More passengers boarded at these ports, bringing together people with very different backgrounds and reasons for traveling.

For some passengers, the journey was a holiday or a business trip. For others, it marked the beginning of a major change in their lives. They carried luggage, personal belongings, and hopes for the future. During the first days at sea, passengers ate meals, walked on deck, and explored the ship. Meanwhile, the crew operated the engines, prepared food, maintained cabins, and guided the liner across the ocean.

What does “maiden voyage” mean?

A maiden voyage is a ship's first official journey after it has been built and prepared for service.

The Iceberg Collision

On the night of April 14, 1912, Titanic was traveling through the North Atlantic. The ship had received warnings about ice in the area. At approximately 11:40 p.m., lookouts spotted an iceberg ahead. The crew attempted to avoid it, but the iceberg struck the ship's starboard side. Water entered several compartments, creating flooding that the ship could not survive.

The crew sent distress messages and began preparing the lifeboats. However, there were not enough lifeboat places for everyone aboard. Titanic sank in the early hours of April 15. More than 1,500 people died, and the surviving passengers and crew were later rescued by the Carpathia. The disaster revealed serious weaknesses in maritime safety and became a turning point in the history of ocean travel.

Why did the watertight compartments fail to save it?

Too many compartments flooded. As the bow sank lower, water passed over the tops of internal bulkheads and spread into additional compartments.
