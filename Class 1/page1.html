How the Great Wall Began

Early Walls | Qin Dynasty | Ming Dynasty

The Early Walls

The Great Wall of China was not originally one continuous wall. Its history began hundreds of years before China became a unified country. During the period known as the Warring States period, different kingdoms controlled different parts of the region. These kingdoms often fought against each other and also faced attacks from groups living north of their borders. To protect important cities, farmland and roads, several states began constructing defensive walls.

These early walls were very different from the stone and brick sections that tourists visit today. Builders often used materials that were available locally. In dry areas they packed layers of earth together, while in mountainous areas stone could be used. The purpose was not to create a famous monument. The walls were practical military structures designed to slow enemies and make borders easier to defend.

The Qin Dynasty

China Becomes Unified

In 221 BCE, Qin Shi Huang became the first emperor of a unified China. After defeating rival kingdoms, he ordered sections of earlier walls to be connected and strengthened along the northern frontier. His government also removed some walls that had previously separated Chinese states because they were no longer necessary.

Large numbers of soldiers and laborers were sent north to work on defensive projects. General Meng Tian is traditionally associated with military campaigns and construction work in this region. Building in remote mountains and deserts was extremely difficult. Workers had to move food, tools and building materials across long distances, often in severe weather conditions.

The Ming Dynasty

Many centuries later, the Ming Dynasty rebuilt and expanded large parts of the Great Wall. The Ming ruled China from 1368 to 1644 and faced military threats from the north. During this period, builders used stronger materials such as bricks and stone in many areas. They also constructed watchtowers, gates, forts and military stations.

Much of the wall that people recognize in photographs today comes from the Ming period. The wall served several purposes. It helped defend territory, controlled movement across the border and allowed soldiers to communicate using signals from watchtowers. Because construction continued under many rulers, the Great Wall is better understood as a large system of fortifications built over centuries rather than one wall created at one moment in history.

Why was the wall built?

It was mainly built for defense and to control movement across China's northern frontier.

Was it built all at once?

No. Different states and dynasties built and rebuilt sections over many centuries.

Which dynasty built the famous sections?

Many of the best-known surviving sections were built or rebuilt during the Ming Dynasty.
