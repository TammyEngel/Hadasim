The Legacy of the Great Wall

The Wall Today | Preservation | A Symbol of China

The Great Wall Today

Today, the Great Wall of China is one of the best-known historical landmarks in the world. Millions of visitors travel to China to see sections of the wall, especially areas that have been restored and made easier to visit. The wall has become an important tourist destination as well as a place for learning about Chinese history.

Visitors can walk along restored sections, enter watchtowers and see the mountains and valleys that once formed part of China's northern frontier. However, the wall is not one perfectly preserved structure. Some areas remain in excellent condition while others are damaged or difficult to recognize.

Preserving the Wall

Protecting a Historic Site

Preserving the Great Wall is challenging because it stretches across a huge area. Wind, rain, plants and erosion can slowly destroy parts of the structure. Human activity can also cause damage. For this reason, historians, archaeologists and conservation workers study different sections and work to protect them.

In 1987, the Great Wall was added to UNESCO's World Heritage List. This recognition emphasizes its importance to world history and culture. Restoration projects attempt to protect important areas while also allowing people to visit and understand the site.

A Symbol of China

The Great Wall has become much more than an old military structure. It is now one of the most recognizable symbols of China. It appears in books, films, advertisements, documentaries and school materials. Images of the wall are often used to represent China's long history.

The wall also reminds people that large historical monuments are usually created over long periods of time. Many rulers, soldiers and workers contributed to it. Different dynasties changed its design and purpose, and parts of it were rebuilt many times.

One popular myth claims that the Great Wall can easily be seen from the Moon with the naked eye. This is not true. Although the wall is extremely long, it is relatively narrow and blends into the surrounding landscape. The myth itself, however, demonstrates how famous the Great Wall has become around the world.

Explore the Legacy

Is the entire wall still standing?

No. Some sections are well preserved, while others are damaged or have disappeared.

Is the Great Wall a World Heritage Site?

Yes. UNESCO added the Great Wall to the World Heritage List in 1987.

Can the wall be seen from the Moon?

No. This is a popular myth. It cannot normally be seen from the Moon with the naked eye.
