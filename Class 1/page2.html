People Behind the Great Wall

Qin Shi Huang | Meng Tian | Workers

Qin Shi Huang

Qin Shi Huang is one of the most important people connected with the early history of the Great Wall. In 221 BCE he became the first emperor to rule a unified China. Before his rule, China had been divided into competing states. After defeating his rivals, Qin Shi Huang created a central government and introduced major political, military and construction projects.

One of his goals was to strengthen China's northern frontier. Existing walls that had been built by earlier states were repaired, expanded or connected. These defenses were intended to protect the empire from raids and attacks. Although Qin Shi Huang did not create the entire Great Wall that exists today, his projects became an important part of its history.

General Meng Tian

A Military Leader

Meng Tian was a general who served Qin Shi Huang. He led military campaigns in northern China and is traditionally associated with the construction and strengthening of defenses in the region. His soldiers fought northern groups and helped secure territory for the Qin Empire.

Organizing construction across such a large and difficult landscape required enormous effort. Supplies needed to be transported to remote areas, soldiers needed food and equipment, and laborers had to work far from major cities. Meng Tian therefore represents both the military and organizational side of the Great Wall's history.

The Workers

The Great Wall was not built only by famous rulers and generals. Thousands of ordinary people played a much larger physical role. Soldiers, farmers, skilled builders, laborers and prisoners all took part in construction during different periods of Chinese history.

Their work was extremely demanding. Some sections of the wall crossed mountains, deserts and isolated regions. Materials had to be moved without modern vehicles or machines. Workers used local earth, stone, wood and, during later periods, bricks. They also built watchtowers, gates, military stations and storage areas.

Life along the wall could be difficult for soldiers as well. Guards spent long periods in remote towers watching the horizon for possible enemies. Signals such as smoke or fire could be used to warn nearby military positions. The people who built and guarded the wall are an important part of its story because the enormous structure could never have existed without their labor.

A Famous Saying

"He who has never been to the Great Wall is not a true man."

This famous line is commonly associated with Mao Zedong and shows how strongly the Great Wall became connected with Chinese national identity.
